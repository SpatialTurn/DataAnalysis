---
title: Learner Profiles
---

These short profiles describe the people this curriculum is written for. They help us decide what
to explain, what to assume, and which examples to use.

### Maya, sociology PhD student

Maya studies housing and neighborhood change. She has survey data with home addresses and a lot of
Census tables, and she has used Excel and a little R in a statistics class. She has never made a map
beyond a screenshot. She wants to put her respondents on a map, compare neighborhoods, and make
figures she can publish. She learns best from short, worked examples on data like her own.

### David, public health master's student

David took one Python course and can read a DataFrame, but GIS terms such as projection or spatial
join are new to him. He wants to measure how far people live from clinics and grocery stores. He
works on a university laptop where he cannot install software, so browser based tools such as
Google Colab suit him well.

### Priya, research librarian

Priya helps faculty and students find and manage data. She is comfortable with metadata and
repositories and has made basic maps in QGIS. She wants to understand enough Python and spatial
analysis to answer patron questions and to teach short workshops herself.

### Dr. Alvarez, archaeology faculty member

Dr. Alvarez has decades of field records with coordinates and wants to bring spatial thinking into
an undergraduate course. He is short on time and wants materials his students can follow on their
own, with clear explanations of why each method is used, not only how.
